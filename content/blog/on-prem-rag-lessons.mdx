## The constraint that shaped everything

The brief was simple to state and hard to satisfy: give people inside the company a conversational way to search internal documents, without any of that data leaving the network. That single constraint — full on-premises operation — ruled out the easiest path (a hosted LLM API with a vector DB in the cloud) and pushed the whole system toward a local stack: **Python, LangChain, and a FAISS vector store**.

## Ingestion was the unglamorous, essential part

Before any retrieval could happen, the documents had to become usable. That meant building an ingestion pipeline with `PyMuPDF` and `pdfplumber` to extract clean text from over 100 internal PDFs — automating a process that used to be manual, error-prone, and slow. Getting this layer right removed copy-paste mistakes from downstream reporting workflows entirely.

## Retrieval quality is the real lever

The instinct when a RAG system feels "off" is to reach for a bigger or better model. In practice, most of the gains here came from earlier in the pipeline: chunking strategy, embedding choices, and how the FAISS index was queried. Tuning that layer is what moved both **query latency** (down roughly 30%) and **retrieval relevance** (up roughly 40%) against the original keyword-search baseline — before the generation model ever entered the picture.
